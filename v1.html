Sustainability & craft

From door to frame.

12 years. 20,000 frames.

All built by hand in Berlin, since a borrowed room in an old swimming pool in 2013.

10,000 of them were doors and floorboards.

Old wood from Berlin and Brandenburg, cut into frames instead of trees.

Some were fishing boats.

We buy old boats in Bali from a family friend and rebuild them as frames.

The offcuts become small frames.

Nothing usable is thrown away. New wood is FSC-certified hardwood.

Nothing is built before you order.

Made to order, so no overstock – and joined to be repaired, not replaced.

Eight ways to frame it.

Every piece asks for something different. Here’s where to start.

  • Passe­partout01A mat around the picture: classic for photos, and small pieces look bigger.
  • Object frame02Floats objects, watercolours and collages, so their edges stay visible.
  • Poster frame03No mat: the picture fills the whole frame.
  • Removable frame04The quickest way: swap the picture at home whenever you like.
  • Antique frame05For special pieces that only come into their own in an old frame.
  • Stretcher frame06For paintings on canvas, large or small, that need stretching.
  • Shadow gap07A floating gap and no glass: for paintings and mounted photos.
  • Mirror08For when your own reflection is the artwork.

Now in the shop · until December

Come for a frame. Stay for the filter.

There’s an espresso bar in the middle of our shop. Until December, Hoa from Antibarist is behind it, Wednesday to Saturday from 8am.

Hoa talks about beans, roast and water the way we talk about wood, glass and mounts. No frame needed – just come by.

Why there’s espresso at Frameworks from August.

It started in a swimming pool

In 2013, when I started, I had nothing to show for myself. No portfolio, no customers, no workshop. What I had was a space in Stattbad Wedding, and a gallery that gave it to me before I’d proved anything.

For anyone who ever saw it: the building went up in 1907, designed by Ludwig Hoffmann, the city architect Berlin owes half its handsome public buildings to. It was a bathhouse for a working-class district where almost nobody had a bathroom of their own, two pools, a large one and a small one. The war damaged it badly, it was rebuilt in the late fifties, and for nearly half a century it was an ordinary swimming hall. In 2001 it closed on hygiene grounds and sat empty.

In 2009 it became a cultural space. The name was a pun: statt Bad — instead of a pool. There were clubs and Boiler Room in the drained basins, exhibitions in the changing rooms, studios in the spaces where towels used to be handed out. Art, music, design, street art, all at once and usually a little too loud. It was one of the places where you could see what Berlin was capable of in those years: take a room with no use left and give it a new one, then see who turns up.

I turned up. Through Open Walls Gallery, who were curating at Stattbad then. They gave me space, for very little money, with no guarantees and no conditions beyond meaning it. They carried me through the first stretch, the part where you still don’t know whether the thing is going to work. Frameworks became official in April 2014. Stattbad is now gone. The city shut it down in 2015 over building code violations, permanently.

What stayed with me

Circular economy usually gets talked about in terms of material. Wood, offcuts, reuse. That’s one half of it, and we take it seriously. The other half is what circulates between people: space, access, the willingness to give somebody a start before they’ve earned it.

What I was given in 2013 didn’t just keep me afloat. It changed what I think a business is for. When you support someone at the beginning, what comes back is hard to put a number on and changes everything anyway. The place that made it possible for me doesn’t exist any more. The thing itself transfers.

Our store is at Mariannenstraße 26, in a part of Kreuzberg that runs on exactly that kind of mix. And it was obvious we should do something with it that doesn’t only serve us.

So we built a bar

A small espresso bar, in the middle of the shop. And we took our time finding the right person for it.

That’s Hoa, who works as Antibarist.

He’s an advisor. He talks about beans, roast and water the way we talk about wood, glass and mounts. He provides opinions that come from experience, and without making the thing bigger than it is. He won’t ask what you usually drink. He’ll ask what you like, and then hand you something better than what you would have ordered. Which is exactly what we’ve been doing with frames for twelve years.

From 1 August

The bar runs for two months, 1 August to the end of September. Wednesday to Saturday, from 8am until closing.

For the first two weeks of August, every hot drink is €3. Espresso, cappuccino, filter, all of it. Normal prices after that.

You can come by for a coffee without needing a frame. You can come with a picture under your arm and do both. And if you’re talking to us about framing, there’s an espresso with it.

FAQs

01/What does framing cost?

Made-to-measure frames start at €56, priced by the length plus width of your piece. See the six sizes

02/How long does it take?

Ready-made frames ship in 2–3 working days. Made-to-measure takes about a week, up to two depending on our suppliers. November and December take longer, so if you have a deadline, tell us first.

03/How do I measure my artwork?

Width and height in mm, we add the rest. The frame overlaps the work by about 5 mm, a mat by about 4 mm. If your print has a white border, measure the whole sheet to keep it, or only the image to hide it.

04/Can I frame an original, a textile or an object?

Yes, in the studio. Bring it by or book a visit and we’ll choose the frame, mat and glass together.

05/What does shipping cost?

Free over €150, and you can always pick up in Kreuzberg. We ship across Germany, Europe and worldwide; for valuable pieces we arrange insured art transport.

06/Can I return a frame?

Yes, within 14 days, undamaged. If something arrives broken, email us photos right away and we’ll fix it or refund you.
